Setup — your reference clip

Read a script out loud, in a quiet room, mic 15–30 cm away, in your normal voice. The recording becomes the voice every backend clones from. Pick the script below — the transcript stored with the clip is the exact text you read, so the backends that need a transcript get it automatically.

Why the script matters, and how long the clip should be. The backends clone from a short prompt — about 10 seconds for Chatterbox, Qwen3 and CosyVoice, up to 30 seconds for VoxCPM2. Read the whole passage, then use the cleanest stretch: a longer recording is material to trim from, not extra voice data the model hears in full. The transcript saved with the clip is the full passage — if you trim the clip afterwards, update the matching refs/<name>.txt to the exact words in the trimmed clip.

Honestly, I never expected to find myself here at 6:45 AM, holding a cup of lukewarm coffee and staring at this screen! Do you have any idea how long this took? It's been—what, three years?—since the V-12 project launched in 2023. We saw a massive 87.5% increase in output, which is just... mind-blowing. But hey, let's not get ahead of ourselves. If we can maintain this momentum, I genuinely believe the next phase will change everything.

Why this script works

  • Questions (upward inflection): “Do you have any idea how long this took?” forces your pitch to rise at the end of the sentence.
  • Excitement (volume and energy): the opening builds to an exclamation point, capturing your high-energy output.
  • Pauses and hesitations: “It’s been—what, three years?—” and “just... mind-blowing” train the model on your cadence, breathing, and natural conversational delays.
  • Alphanumeric data: 6:45 AM, V-12, 2023, and 87.5% teach the model how you pronounce numbers, acronyms, and symbols.
  • Intimacy (lower register): “But hey, let’s not get ahead of ourselves” drops your voice into a lower, calmer register.
The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog. A king ruled the state in the early days. The ship was torn apart on the sharp reef. Sickness kept him home the third week. The wide road shimmered in the hot sun.

Why this script works

  • Phonetically balanced: the sounds of English in roughly the proportions they appear in daily speech (IEEE Harvard Sentences, List 5, 1965 revised list).
  • Short standalone sentences — easy to read one at a time, naturally, without slipping into a “reading” voice.
  • “The quick brown fox…” is a pangram (every letter of the alphabet), not a Harvard sentence — included as a useful read-aloud line.
  • Stand up, use your hands to talk, and smile when the text calls for it — the model picks up the micro-changes in your vocal cords.
